# Introduction to EMG for Students

Electromyography (EMG) records the electrical activity of muscles. In a biomedical signal processing course, EMG is a classic example of a noisy, non-stationary signal that requires both physiology knowledge and careful numerical processing.

## Why EMG matters

EMG is used in:

- rehabilitation engineering;
- prosthetic control;
- sports biomechanics;
- gesture recognition;
- fatigue monitoring.

## What a typical EMG signal looks like

A raw EMG signal is usually a rapidly changing waveform with bursts of activity and periods of lower amplitude. The signal is often affected by:

- electrode noise;
- motion artifacts;
- power-line interference;
- baseline drift.

## Learning goals

By working through this repository, students should be able to:

1. understand the basic morphology of EMG signals;
2. apply common preprocessing methods;
3. extract meaningful features;
4. interpret how those features relate to muscle activation.
